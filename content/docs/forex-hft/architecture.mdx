## Processes

| Process | Does | Cannot |
|---|---|---|
| Recorder | Writes every price update to an append-only, checksummed store | Place an order |
| Trader | Runs the loop above for every sleeve, against two books | Trade above a sleeve's stage, or past the risk gate |
| Scorer | Scores models in a process of its own, with a timeout and restart | Hold a venue connection |
| Weekend learning | Builds datasets, trains candidates, validates, registers | Promote outside the written rule |
| Publisher | Builds and signs the status snapshot | Send free text |

Keeping the recorder apart from the trader means a trading fault cannot stop the recording, and data keeps
accumulating.

## Design choices

- **Integer prices everywhere.** No floating-point money, so a replay gives the same result on any machine.
- **Deterministic replay.** A recorded day can be run again and must reproduce the same decisions.
- **Sleeves are pure functions.** They take state and a quote and return intents; they hold no connection.
- **One venue interface.** The paper broker, the simulator and the fake broker used in tests all sit behind it.
- **Models scored as whole numbers.** A trained model is stored and scored in integers; where rounding could
  change a decision, the scorer abstains.
- **Journals are hash-chained.** A changed or missing entry is detectable.

## Test layers

1. Unit and property tests on each package.
2. The fake broker, with faults injected: disconnects, rejects, partial fills, duplicates.
3. Replay parity on recordings the tests make.
4. Scans of the source: only the order manager may place or cancel; only two venues are registered.

None of these is the real market. See [what has been measured](/forex-hft/measured).
